\begin{definition}
\label{def:rational-number}
A real number $r$ is rational if, and only if, $r=\frac{a}{b}$ for some integers $a,b$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
The sum of any two rational numbers is rational.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $a,b \in \mathbb{Q}$. Observe that $a+b$ is integer. Now,

\[
a+b=\frac{a+b}{1}
\]

Since $a+b$ can be expressed as a ratio of two integers, namely $a+b$ and $1$, by
definition of a rational number (Definition \ref{def:rational-number}),
$a+b$ is rational.
\end{proof}
