\textbf{Proposition:} If a sum of two integers is even, then one of the summands is even.

\textbf{Proof:} Let $r=a+b$ be the sum of two integers $a$ and $b$.

Choose $a$ and $b$ to be odd. Then, $a=2m+1$ and $b=2n+1$ for some integers $m$ and $n$. So,

\begin{align*}
r&=a+b\\
&=(2m+1)+(2n+1)\\
&=2m+2n+2\\
&=2(m+n+1)
\end{align*}

Thus, $r$ is even. Therefore, we found integers, namely $a$ and $b$, such that
their sum is even, but neither of them is even. This contradicts the proposition
which states that at least of the summands must be even. Hence, the proposition is false.
