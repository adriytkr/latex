\begin{proposition}
Any integer greater than $1$ is divisible by a prime number.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $P(n)$: "$p|n$" where $p$ is prime and $n \ge 2$.

\textbf{Base Step:} $n=2$

$2$ is divisible by $2$, which is a prime number. Thus,
P(2) holds.

\textbf{Inductive Step:}

Suppose $P(i)$ is true for every integer $i$ from $2$ through $k$.

\textbf{Case $k+1$ is prime:}

Then, $k+1$ is divibible by $k+1$, which is prime.

\textbf{Case $k+1$ is not prime:}

Then, $k+1=ab$ for some integers $a$, $b$ with $1<a<k+1$ and
$1<b<k+1$. Thus, P(a) is true. Then, $a=p_ak_a$ for
some prime number $p_a$ and integer $k_a$. Hence,

\begin{align*}
k+1&=ab\\
&=p_ak_ab\\
&=p_a(k_ab)
\end{align*}

Hence, $k+1$ is divisible by a prime number, namely $p_a$.

In both cases, $P(k+1)$ holds. Therefore, any integer greater than $1$
is divisible by a prime number.

\end{proof}
