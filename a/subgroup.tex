\begin{problem}
Let $D$ denote the set of all differentiable functions on $\mathbb{R}$.
Let $G=\langle D,+ \rangle$ where $+$ denotes the usual addition.
Let $H=\{f \in D \mid \frac{df}{dx} \text{ is constant}\}$.
Prove that $H$ is a subgroup of $G$.
\end{problem}
\begin{proof}
Let $f,g \in H$. Then, $\frac{df}{dx}=c_f$ for some constant $c_f$. Then,
by integrating both sides, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\int \frac{df}{dx}&=\int c_f\\
f(x)&=c_fx+k_f
\end{align*}
where $k_f$ is a constant.

Similarly, $g(x)=c_g(x)+k_g$ where $k_g$ is a constant.

Observe that
\begin{align*}
(f+g)(x)&=(c_fx+k_f)+(c_gx+k_g)\\
&=(c_f+c_g)x+(k_f+k_g)
\end{align*}

By differentiating both sides, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d}{dx}(f+g)(x)=c_f+c_g
\end{equation*}

Since $c_f+c_g$ is a constant, $f+g \in H$. Hence, $H$ is closed under addition.

Notice that $0 \in H$ since $\frac{d}{dx}0=0$. Then, $f+0=0+f=f$.
Thus, $H$ has an identity.

Furthermore, note that $-f(x)=-c_fx-k_f$, and $\frac{d}{dx}{[-f(x)]}=-c_f$.
Hence, since $-c_f$ is a constant, we have $-f \in H$.

Now,
\begin{align*}
f(x)+(-f(x))&=(c_fx+k_f)+(-c_fx-k_f)\\
&=(c_f-c_f)x+(k_f-k_f)\\
&=0
\end{align*}

Because $0$ is the identity of $H$ and there exists another function,
namely $-f$, such that $f+(-f)=0$, it follows that $f$ has an inverse.

Therefore, $H$ is a subgroup of $G$.
\end{proof}
